== class-6/maths/patterns-in-mathematics
I: Mathematics is the study of patterns. Number sequences like odd numbers (1, 3, 5 …), square numbers (1, 4, 9 …) and triangular numbers (1, 3, 6 …) follow rules we can discover, picture with dots, and use to predict what comes next.
K: Adding consecutive odd numbers starting from 1 gives square numbers: 1 + 3 + 5 = 9 = 3².
K: Triangular numbers: 1, 3, 6, 10, 15 … (add 2, 3, 4, 5 …).
K: Powers of 2: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 … (each doubles).
E: Find the sum 1 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 9 + 11 without adding one by one.
S: These are the first 6 odd numbers.
S: Sum of the first n odd numbers = n × n.
A: 6 × 6 = 36
E: Find the next two terms: 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, __, __
S: Differences are 2, 3, 4, 5 → next differences are 6 and 7.
S: 15 + 6 = 21, 21 + 7 = 28
A: 21, 28
P: Next term: 2, 4, 8, 16, __
R: 32
P: Sum of the first 10 odd numbers?
R: 100
P: Next term: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, __
R: 36

== class-6/maths/lines-and-angles
I: A point marks a position; a line segment has two endpoints; a ray has one endpoint and goes on in one direction; a line goes on in both directions. Two rays from a common point (vertex) form an angle, measured in degrees with a protractor.
K: Acute < 90°, right = 90°, obtuse between 90° and 180°, straight = 180°, reflex between 180° and 360°.
K: A full turn is 360°.
K: Name an angle with its vertex in the middle: ∠ABC has vertex B.
E: What is the angle between the hands of a clock at 4 o'clock?
S: Each hour mark is 360° ÷ 12 = 30° apart.
S: From 12 to 4 is 4 marks: 4 × 30°.
A: 120° (obtuse)
E: An angle measures 250°. What type is it?
S: It is more than 180° but less than 360°.
A: Reflex angle
P: How many degrees apart are two adjacent hour marks on a clock?
R: 30°
P: Type of angle: 90°?
R: Right angle
P: In ∠PQR, which point is the vertex?
R: Q

== class-6/maths/number-play

K: A palindrome reads the same forwards and backwards: 353, 4884.
K: Digit sum of 347 = 3 + 4 + 7 = 14.
K: Kaprekar's constant: repeatedly subtracting the smallest from the largest arrangement of a 4-digit number reaches 6174.
E: Start with 3524. Arrange digits largest and smallest, then subtract.
S: Largest: 5432; smallest: 2345.
S: 5432 − 2345 = 3087
A: 3087 (repeating the process will reach 6174)
E: Reverse 67 and add: is the result a palindrome?
S: 67 + 76 = 143; not a palindrome. Repeat: 143 + 341 = 484.
A: Yes, after two steps: 484
P: Is 1331 a palindrome?
R: Yes
P: Digit sum of 9,081?
R: 18
P: Largest 3-digit number using 4, 0, 9 once each?
R: 940

== class-6/maths/prime-time
I: A prime number has exactly two factors, 1 and itself (2, 3, 5, 7, 11 …). Composite numbers have more than two factors. Every composite number can be written as a product of primes (prime factorisation). Divisibility rules help us test factors quickly.
K: Divisible by 2: last digit even; by 3: digit sum divisible by 3; by 5: ends in 0 or 5.
K: Divisible by 4: last two digits divisible by 4; by 9: digit sum divisible by 9; by 10: ends in 0.
K: Co-prime numbers have no common factor except 1 (e.g. 8 and 15).
E: Prime factorise 84.
S: 84 = 2 × 42 = 2 × 2 × 21 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7
A: 84 = 2² × 3 × 7
E: Is 2,346 divisible by 3 and by 4?
S: Digit sum = 2 + 3 + 4 + 6 = 15, divisible by 3 → yes for 3.
S: Last two digits 46; 46 is not divisible by 4 → no for 4.
A: Divisible by 3, not by 4
P: Is 91 prime?
R: No (7 × 13)
P: Prime factorise 60.
R: 2² × 3 × 5
P: Are 9 and 16 co-prime?
R: Yes

== class-6/maths/perimeter-and-area
I: Perimeter is the total length of the boundary of a closed figure. Area is the amount of surface it covers, measured in square units. For rectangles and squares we have simple formulas; for triangles, area is half the rectangle around it.
K: Rectangle: P = 2(l + b), A = l × b
K: Square: P = 4s, A = s × s
K: Triangle: A = ½ × base × height
E: A rectangular park is 60 m by 40 m. Find the cost of fencing at ₹25 per metre.
S: Perimeter = 2 × (60 + 40) = 200 m.
S: Cost = 200 × ₹25
A: ₹5,000
E: A triangle has base 12 cm and height 5 cm. Find its area.
S: Area = ½ × 12 × 5
A: 30 sq cm
P: Area of a square of side 15 cm?
R: 225 sq cm
P: A rectangle has area 96 sq m and length 12 m. Breadth?
R: 8 m
P: Perimeter of an equilateral triangle of side 9 cm?
R: 27 cm

== class-6/maths/fractions
I: A fraction represents a part of a whole or a point on the number line. We compare, add and subtract fractions by making denominators equal (using the LCM). Mixed numbers like 2¾ combine a whole number and a fraction.
K: Equivalent fractions: 2/3 = 4/6 = 8/12.
K: Simplest form: divide numerator and denominator by their HCF.
K: Mixed to improper: 2¾ = (2 × 4 + 3)/4 = 11/4.

A: 17/12 = 1 5/12

S: HCF of 18 and 24 is 6.
S: 18 ÷ 6 = 3, 24 ÷ 6 = 4
A: 3/4
P: 5/6 − 1/4 = ?
R: 7/12
P: Convert 17/5 to a mixed number.
R: 3 2/5

== class-6/maths/symmetry
I: A figure has line symmetry if a line divides it into two mirror-image halves. It has rotational symmetry if it looks the same after a turn of less than 360°. The number of times it matches in one full turn is its order of rotational symmetry.
K: Square: 4 lines of symmetry, rotational order 4.
K: Equilateral triangle: 3 lines, order 3.
K: Circle: infinite lines of symmetry.
E: How many lines of symmetry does a regular hexagon have?
S: A regular polygon with n sides has n lines of symmetry.
A: 6
E: What is the order of rotational symmetry of the letter S?
S: Turning S by 180° gives the same shape.
A: 2
P: Lines of symmetry in a rectangle (not square)?
R: 2
P: Does the letter H have line symmetry?
R: Yes (two lines)
P: Order of rotational symmetry of a square?
R: 4

== class-6/science/the-wonderful-world-of-science
I: Science is a way of understanding the world by asking questions, observing carefully, making guesses (hypotheses), testing them with experiments and drawing conclusions. Everyone can think like a scientist.
K: Steps: observe → question → hypothesis → experiment → conclusion.
K: A fair test changes only one thing at a time.
K: Record observations honestly, even if they don't match your guess.
E: Does a plant grow better in sunlight or in the dark? Design a fair test.
S: Take two identical pots with the same plant, soil and water.
S: Keep one in sunlight and one in a dark cupboard; observe for a week.
A: Change only light; keep everything else the same, then compare growth.
E: Why must we change only one thing in an experiment?
S: If many things change, we can't tell which one caused the result.
A: So we know exactly what caused the change we see.
P: What is an educated guess in science called?
R: A hypothesis
P: What is the first step of the scientific method?
R: Observation
P: What is a test that changes only one factor called?
R: A fair test

K: Magnetic materials: iron, nickel, cobalt. Non-magnetic: wood, plastic, glass, copper.
K: Like poles repel; unlike poles attract.
K: Attraction is strongest at the poles.
E: Two bar magnets are placed with their north poles facing each other. What happens?
S: Like poles face each other.
A: They repel (push away).
E: Why does a compass needle point north?
S: The needle is a tiny magnet.
S: Earth behaves like a giant magnet.
A: The magnetic needle aligns north–south along Earth's magnetic field.
P: Will a magnet attract an aluminium can?
R: No
P: Where is a magnet's attraction strongest?
R: At its poles
P: North pole near south pole: attract or repel?
R: Attract

== class-6/science/measurement-of-length-and-motion

R: Circular (and periodic)

== class-6/science/a-journey-through-states-of-water
I: Water exists as solid (ice), liquid (water) and gas (water vapour). Heating and cooling change its state: melting, evaporation, condensation and freezing. These changes drive the water cycle that brings rain.
K: Melting: ice → water; freezing: water → ice.
K: Evaporation: water → vapour (faster with heat, wind, large surface).
K: Condensation: vapour → water droplets (dew, clouds, drops on a cold glass).
E: Why do droplets form outside a glass of ice water?
S: Water vapour in the air touches the cold glass.
A: The vapour cools and condenses into liquid droplets.
E: Why do clothes dry faster on a sunny, windy day?
S: Heat and wind speed up evaporation.
A: Evaporation is faster with more heat and moving air.
P: Change of water into vapour is called?
R: Evaporation
P: How do clouds form?
R: Water vapour rises, cools and condenses into tiny droplets
P: Change of ice into water is called?
R: Melting

E: Why is Indian Standard Time ahead of Greenwich time by 5 h 30 min?

P: Latitude of the Equator?
R: 0°
P: Where does the Prime Meridian pass?
R: Greenwich, London
P: How many degrees does Earth rotate in one hour?
R: 15°

== class-6/social-science/grassroots-democracy-local-government
I: Local government works closest to people. In villages, the Gram Panchayat (headed by the Sarpanch) handles roads, water, sanitation and schools. In towns and cities, municipal bodies (headed by a Mayor or chairperson) do similar work. The Gram Sabha includes all adult voters of the village.
K: Three-tier Panchayati Raj: Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti (block), Zila Parishad (district).
K: Gram Sabha: all adults (18+) of the village meet to discuss plans.
K: Municipal Corporation for big cities; Municipal Council for towns.
E: Who heads a Gram Panchayat?
S: This person is elected by the village.
A: The Sarpanch (Pradhan)
E: Where should villagers complain about a broken hand-pump?
S: Local water supply is a Panchayat duty.
A: To the Gram Panchayat (or raise it in the Gram Sabha)
P: Who can attend the Gram Sabha?
R: All adult voters of the village
P: Who heads a Municipal Corporation?
R: The Mayor
P: Name the three tiers of Panchayati Raj.
R: Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti, Zila Parishad

K: Primary: farming, fishing, mining, forestry.
K: Secondary: manufacturing, construction.
K: Tertiary: services such as trade, transport, health, education.
E: Classify: a baker who makes bread from flour.
S: The baker turns a raw material into a new product.
A: Secondary activity
E: Classify: a doctor treating patients.
S: The doctor provides a service, not a product.
A: Tertiary activity
P: Fishing is which type of activity?
R: Primary
P: A car factory is which type?
R: Secondary
P: A bus conductor's work is which type?
R: Tertiary

== class-6/social-science/the-value-of-work

R: Yes — all honest work deserves respect

== class-6/computer/computer-systems

K: RAM is temporary (volatile); ROM and storage are permanent.
K: Units: 1 KB = 1024 bytes, 1 MB = 1024 KB, 1 GB = 1024 MB.
E: Is Windows system software or application software?

A: System software (operating system)
E: Why is unsaved work lost when power goes off?

R: Central Processing Unit
P: Is a web browser system or application software?
R: Application software
P: How many MB make 1 GB?
R: 1024

== class-6/computer/spreadsheets-basics

K: Formulas begin with =; functions like SUM, AVERAGE, MAX, MIN.
K: A range like B2:B6 means all cells from B2 to B6.

P: With what symbol does every formula begin?

== class-6/computer/algorithms-and-flowcharts
I: An algorithm is a step-by-step set of instructions to solve a problem. A flowchart shows an algorithm with symbols: oval (start/end), parallelogram (input/output), rectangle (process) and diamond (decision).
K: Oval: start/stop; parallelogram: input/output.
K: Rectangle: process; diamond: decision (yes/no).
K: Good algorithms are clear, ordered and finish.
E: Write an algorithm to find the larger of two numbers.
S: Step 1: Start. Step 2: Input A and B.
S: Step 3: If A > B, print A; else print B. Step 4: Stop.
A: Start → input A, B → if A > B print A else print B → stop
E: Which flowchart symbol is used for "Is it raining?"
S: It is a yes/no question.
A: Diamond (decision)
P: Which symbol shows start or end?
R: Oval
P: Which symbol shows input or output?
R: Parallelogram
P: What is a step-by-step solution called?
R: An algorithm
