== class-7/maths/triangles-and-their-properties
I: The angles of a triangle add up to 180°. An exterior angle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles. The sum of any two sides is greater than the third side. In a right triangle, Pythagoras' theorem holds: hypotenuse² = base² + height².
K: Angle sum property: ∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°.
K: Exterior angle = sum of interior opposite angles.
K: Pythagoras: c² = a² + b² (c = hypotenuse).

== class-7/maths/congruence-of-triangles
I: Congruent figures have exactly the same shape and size. Two triangles are congruent if they satisfy one of these criteria: SSS, SAS, ASA or RHS. Corresponding parts of congruent triangles are equal (CPCT).
K: SSS: three sides equal.
K: SAS: two sides and the included angle equal; ASA: two angles and the included side.
K: RHS: right angle, hypotenuse and one side equal.
E: △ABC and △PQR have AB = PQ, BC = QR and ∠B = ∠Q. Are they congruent?
S: Two sides and the angle between them are equal.
A: Yes, by SAS
E: Two right triangles have equal hypotenuses and one equal side. Which rule proves congruence?
S: Right angle + hypotenuse + side.
A: RHS
P: What does CPCT stand for?
R: Corresponding parts of congruent triangles
P: Is AAA a congruence rule?
R: No (it gives similar, not necessarily congruent, triangles)
P: Three sides of one triangle equal three sides of another. Rule?
R: SSS
