== class-7/english/grammar-tenses-and-their-use
I: Choosing the right tense makes meaning clear. Simple tenses state facts and habits; continuous tenses show ongoing actions; perfect tenses show completed actions linked to another time; perfect continuous shows duration.
K: Present perfect continuous: has/have been + -ing (I have been waiting for an hour).
K: Past perfect: had + V3 for the earlier of two past actions.
K: Future perfect: will have + V3 (By 6 p.m. I will have finished).
E: Fill in with "study": "She __ for three hours and is tired now."
S: Action began in the past, continued till now, with duration.
A: has been studying
E: Fill in with "complete": "By next Monday, we __ the project."
S: Action will be finished before a future time.
A: will have completed
P: Fill in with "go": "When I arrived, he __ already __." (past perfect)
R: had … gone
P: Fill in with "wait": "They __ since morning." (present perfect continuous)
R: have been waiting
P: Fill in with "boil": "Water __ at 100 °C." (fact)
R: boils

== class-7/english/grammar-active-and-passive-voice
I: The passive voice focuses on the action or receiver rather than the doer. It is formed with a form of "be" + past participle. The passive is common in science and news writing.
K: Simple present: is/are + V3; simple past: was/were + V3.
K: Continuous: is being / was being + V3; perfect: has been / had been + V3.
K: Future: will be + V3.
E: Change to passive: "The workers are building a bridge."
S: Present continuous → is being + V3; bridge (singular).
A: A bridge is being built by the workers.
E: Change to passive: "They have finished the work."
S: Present perfect → has been + V3.
A: The work has been finished (by them).
P: Change to passive: "Someone will clean the room."
R: The room will be cleaned.
P: Change to passive: "The police caught the thief."
R: The thief was caught by the police.
P: Change to active: "The letter was written by Asha."
R: Asha wrote the letter.

== class-7/english/grammar-direct-and-indirect-speech
I: Indirect speech reports what someone said. Change pronouns, move tenses one step back (if the reporting verb is past), and change time words (now → then, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day).
K: Statements: said that; questions: asked if/whether or asked + wh-word.
K: Commands: told/ordered + to + verb; requests: requested + to + verb.
K: Time words: here → there, this → that, yesterday → the previous day.
E: Report: Mother said, "Close the window."
S: Command → told + to + verb.
A: Mother told me to close the window.
E: Report: Ravi said, "I will come tomorrow."
S: I → he; will → would; tomorrow → the next day.
A: Ravi said that he would come the next day.
P: Report: She asked, "Why are you late?"
R: She asked me why I was late.
P: Report: He said, "I am reading now."
R: He said that he was reading then.
P: Report: The teacher said, "Please be quiet."
R: The teacher requested us to be quiet.

== class-7/english/grammar-clauses
I: A clause is a group of words with a subject and a verb. A main (independent) clause makes sense on its own; a subordinate (dependent) clause does not and begins with words like because, when, if, who, which, that.
K: Main clause: "I stayed home" — complete thought.
K: Subordinate clause: "because it was raining" — incomplete alone.
K: Relative clauses start with who, which, that and describe a noun.
E: Identify the clauses: "When the bell rang, the students left."
S: "the students left" makes sense alone.
S: "When the bell rang" depends on it.
A: Main: the students left; Subordinate: When the bell rang
E: Combine using "who": "I met a girl. She plays the violin."
S: "who" refers back to the girl.
A: I met a girl who plays the violin.
P: Is "because he was tired" a main or subordinate clause?
R: Subordinate
P: Combine using "which": "This is the book. It won an award."
R: This is the book which won an award.
P: What must every clause have?
R: A subject and a verb

== class-7/english/writing-formal-and-informal-letters
I: Informal letters are friendly and personal; formal letters are polite, to the point and follow a fixed format. Choose tone and language to suit the reader.
K: Formal: sender's address, date, receiver's address, subject, Sir/Madam, body, Yours faithfully/sincerely.
K: Informal: address, date, Dear …, body, Yours lovingly/affectionately.
K: Formal letters avoid slang and contractions (don't → do not).
E: Write the opening of a letter to the editor about stray animals on roads.
S: Include "The Editor", the newspaper, a subject line and "Sir".
A: The Editor, The Daily Times, Pune. Subject: Menace of stray animals on roads. Sir, Through your esteemed newspaper I wish to draw attention to …
E: Which closing suits a letter to your cousin?
S: The tone is friendly and personal.
A: Yours lovingly / Your affectionate cousin
P: Should a formal letter use slang?
R: No
P: What line states the purpose of a formal letter?
R: The subject line
P: Closing for a letter to the Principal?
R: Yours sincerely / Yours obediently

== class-7/english/writing-story-and-report
I: A story needs characters, setting, conflict and resolution, told with vivid details and dialogue. A report gives facts about an event (what, when, where, who, how) in the past tense, with a heading and the writer's name.
K: Story: hook opening, rising action, climax, resolution.
K: Report: headline, byline (name), date and place, facts in order.
K: Reports are factual and use passive voice often ("A function was organised").
E: Write the first line of a report on your school's sports day.
S: Include when, where and what.
A: Sunrise School, Jaipur, 15 December: The annual sports day was held on the school ground with great enthusiasm.
E: Write a hook to begin a story.
S: Start with action, a question or a surprising line.
A: The moment the lights went out, we heard footsteps above us.
P: Which tense is used in a report?
R: Past tense
P: What part of a story solves the problem?
R: Resolution
P: What is a byline in a report?
R: The writer's name
