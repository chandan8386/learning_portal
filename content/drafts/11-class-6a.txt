== class-6/english/prose-and-poetry-fables-and-folk-tales
I: Fables are short stories, often with animals as characters, that end with a moral (lesson). Folk tales are traditional stories passed down by word of mouth, like the Panchatantra and Jataka tales. They teach wisdom in a simple, memorable way.
K: A fable has a clear moral, often stated at the end.
K: Animals in fables behave like people and show human qualities.
K: Folk tales change a little as they are retold across regions.
E: In the fable of the crow and the fox, the fox praises the crow's voice and the crow drops its cheese. What is the moral?
S: The fox flatters the crow only to get the cheese.
S: The crow believes the praise and loses its food.
A: Do not trust flatterers.
E: Why do fables use animals instead of people?
S: Animals have well-known traits: the fox is clever, the lion is strong, the tortoise is slow.
A: Animals make the lesson simple and easy to remember, and readers can see human faults without feeling targeted.
P: What is the lesson at the end of a fable called?
R: The moral
P: Name a famous collection of Indian fables.
R: Panchatantra (also Jataka tales, Hitopadesha)
P: How are folk tales traditionally passed on?
R: By word of mouth, from one generation to the next

== class-6/english/prose-and-poetry-friendship
I: Stories and poems about friendship show loyalty, trust, forgiveness and sharing. Reading them, we notice how characters change and what makes a true friend.
K: Character traits are shown through actions and words.
K: Conflict between friends often leads to a lesson.
K: A theme is the big idea of a text, such as "true friends stand by each other".
E: Two friends see a bear. One climbs a tree and leaves the other, who pretends to be dead. Later he says, "The bear told me not to trust a friend who leaves you in danger." What is the theme?
S: One friend abandoned the other in trouble.
A: A true friend helps in times of danger.
E: How can you tell a character is loyal?
S: Look at what they do when things get difficult.
A: They keep their promises and stay by their friend even when it is hard.
P: What is the big idea of a story called?
R: The theme
P: Give one quality of a good friend shown in stories.
R: Loyalty, honesty, kindness or forgiveness
P: How are character traits shown in a text?
R: Through the character's actions, words and thoughts

K: Texts on nature often carry a message about conservation.

== class-6/english/grammar-tenses
I: Tenses show the time of an action. Each of present, past and future has four forms: simple, continuous, perfect and perfect continuous. The perfect tense links a past action to another time: "I have finished my homework."
K: Present perfect: has/have + past participle (She has eaten).
K: Past perfect: had + past participle (The train had left before we arrived).
K: Future simple: will + verb; future continuous: will be + -ing.
E: Fill in with "finish": "I __ my homework, so I can play now."
S: The action is complete and matters now → present perfect.
A: have finished
E: Fill in with "leave": "When we reached the station, the train __."
S: One past action happened before another past action → past perfect.
A: had left
P: Fill in with "live": "They __ in Pune since 2019."
R: have lived / have been living
P: Fill in with "rain": "It __ when I woke up." (past continuous)
R: was raining
P: Change to future: "She visits her aunt."
R: She will visit her aunt.

== class-6/english/grammar-determiners-and-modals
I: Determiners come before nouns to show which or how much: a, an, the, this, those, some, any, many, much, few, little, each, every. Modals are helping verbs that show ability, permission, possibility or duty: can, could, may, might, must, should, will, would.
K: some — positive sentences; any — questions and negatives.
K: few/many — countable; little/much — uncountable.
K: can (ability), may (permission/possibility), must (necessity), should (advice).
E: Fill in: "There isn't __ milk left." (some/any)
S: The sentence is negative → any.
A: any
E: Fill in: "You __ wear a helmet while riding." (can/must)
S: It is a rule for safety → necessity.
A: must
P: Fill in: "I have __ friends in this city." (few/little)
R: few
P: Fill in: "__ I come in, please?" (May/Must)
R: May
P: Fill in: "You __ drink more water; it's good for you." (should/can)
R: should

== class-6/english/grammar-voice-and-narration-basics
I: In the active voice the doer comes first; in the passive voice the receiver comes first. In reported (indirect) speech we report what someone said without their exact words, usually changing pronouns and tenses.
K: Passive: object + be + past participle (+ by + doer).
K: Reported speech: said → said that; tense moves back (is → was).
K: Questions: asked if/whether (yes-no) or asked + wh-word.
E: Change to passive: "The gardener waters the plants."
S: Object "the plants" becomes the subject; present tense, plural → are watered.
A: The plants are watered by the gardener.
E: Report: Riya asked, "Are you coming?"
S: Yes-no question → asked if; you → I/he/she; are → was/were.
A: Riya asked if I was coming.
P: Change to passive: "Tom broke the vase."
R: The vase was broken by Tom.
P: Report: He said, "I am busy."
R: He said that he was busy.
P: Report: She asked, "Where do you live?"
R: She asked where I lived.

== class-6/english/writing-letter-notice-and-paragraph
I: Formal letters are written to officials (principal, editor) with a set format and polite tone. Notices inform many people briefly. A good paragraph has a topic sentence, supporting details and a conclusion.
K: Formal letter: sender's address, date, receiver's address, subject, salutation, body, complimentary close, name.
K: Notice: organisation, NOTICE, date, heading, body (what, when, where, who), name and designation.
K: Paragraph: one main idea, 100–120 words, linking words (firstly, moreover, finally).
E: Write the subject line of a letter to the principal asking for a class library.
S: The subject states the purpose in a few words.
A: Subject: Request to set up a library in Class 6
E: Which linking word shows an extra point: "however" or "moreover"?
S: "However" shows contrast; "moreover" adds information.
A: moreover
P: What closing do we use in a formal letter?
R: Yours sincerely / Yours faithfully
P: Name the four W's a notice should answer.
R: What, when, where, who
P: What does a topic sentence do?
R: States the main idea of the paragraph

== class-6/english/reading-comprehension
I: Unseen passages test understanding. Read the questions first, then the passage, underline key lines and answer in your own words. Some questions ask for facts, some for inferences, and some for vocabulary.
K: Skim for the main idea; scan for specific facts.
K: Inference questions need reasoning, not copying.
K: Vocabulary: find a word in the passage that means …
E: "Despite the heavy rain, the farmers were relieved; their parched fields finally had water." Why were the farmers relieved?
S: "Parched" means very dry; rain solved their problem.
A: Because their dry fields finally got water.
E: Find a word in the sentence that means "very dry".
S: Look for the word describing the fields.
A: parched
P: What is reading quickly to get the main idea called?
R: Skimming
P: What is searching a passage for a specific fact called?
R: Scanning
P: Should inference answers be copied word for word?
R: No — explain using clues in your own words

== class-6/hindi/essay-letter-and-dialogue-writing
I: निबंध में विचार क्रमबद्ध होते हैं। पत्र औपचारिक और अनौपचारिक होते हैं। संवाद लेखन में दो या अधिक लोगों की बातचीत स्वाभाविक, छोटे वाक्यों में लिखी जाती है।
K: निबंध: भूमिका, विषय-विस्तार (2–3 अनुच्छेद), उपसंहार
K: संवाद: पात्र का नाम, फिर उसका कथन; भाषा सरल और पात्र के अनुकूल
K: औपचारिक पत्र में "विषय" अवश्य लिखें।
E: दुकानदार और ग्राहक के बीच दो पंक्तियों का संवाद लिखो।
S: ग्राहक कुछ माँगे, दुकानदार जवाब दे।
A: ग्राहक: भैया, एक किलो सेब कितने के हैं? दुकानदार: जी, एक सौ बीस रुपये किलो।
E: "समय का महत्व" निबंध की भूमिका लिखो।
S: विषय का परिचय और उसका महत्व बताओ।
A: समय सबसे क़ीमती धन है। बीता हुआ समय कभी लौटकर नहीं आता, इसलिए जो समय का सदुपयोग करता है वही सफल होता है।
P: संवाद की भाषा कैसी होनी चाहिए?
R: सरल, स्वाभाविक और पात्र के अनुकूल
P: निबंध के विषय-विस्तार में कितने अनुच्छेद ठीक हैं?
R: 2–3
P: औपचारिक पत्र में कौन-सी पंक्ति ज़रूरी है जो अनौपचारिक में नहीं होती?
R: विषय
